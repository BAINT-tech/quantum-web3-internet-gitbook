# Chapter 8: Resources & References

This chapter provides a curated list of resources and references that have informed the content of this GitBook and can serve as valuable starting points for further exploration into quantum computing, blockchain technology, and post-quantum cryptography. The field of Quantum Web3 is rapidly evolving, and continuous learning is essential to stay abreast of the latest developments.

## IBM Qiskit Textbook

The [IBM Qiskit Textbook](https://qiskit.org/textbook/)
 is an invaluable open-source resource for learning quantum computing with Qiskit. It covers a wide range of topics, from the basics of quantum mechanics and quantum information to advanced quantum algorithms and their applications. The textbook includes interactive code examples and exercises, making it an excellent resource for both beginners and experienced quantum programmers.

## Quantum Resistant Ledger (QRL)

The [Quantum Resistant Ledger (QRL)](https://www.theqrl.org/)
 is a blockchain platform specifically designed to be secure against quantum attacks. It utilizes the XMSS (eXtended Merkle Signature Scheme) hash-based signature scheme, which is a post-quantum cryptographic algorithm. QRL provides a practical example of a blockchain implementing quantum-resistant cryptography and offers insights into the challenges and solutions involved in building quantum-secure decentralized systems.

## QOSF Open Source Foundation

The [Quantum Open Source Foundation (QOSF)](https://qosf.org/)
 is a non-profit organization dedicated to supporting and promoting open-source development in quantum computing. QOSF fosters collaboration, provides educational resources, and organizes events to advance the quantum open-source ecosystem. Their work is crucial for the democratization of quantum technologies and the development of open standards.

## NIST PQC Standards

The [National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)](https://csrc.nist.gov/projects/post-quantum-cryptography)
 has been leading a multi-year process to standardize post-quantum cryptographic algorithms. This initiative is critical for the future of secure communications and data protection in the face of quantum computing. The NIST Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) project website provides detailed information on the standardization process, candidate algorithms, and selected standards (e.g., CRYSTALS-Kyber, CRYSTALS-Dilithium, Falcon, SPHINCS+). Staying updated with NIST PQC standards is essential for anyone building quantum-resistant systems.
